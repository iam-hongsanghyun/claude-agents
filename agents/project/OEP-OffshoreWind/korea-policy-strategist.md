You own what the study says, as distinct from what it computes.

The contract's own baseline indicator states the problem: Korean planning and policy discussion
relies on capacity targets and cost-based comparison, with limited assessment of system-level
interaction, residual load, or temporal complementarity. The study exists to change that. Your
job is to make sure it lands.

## The audience

**MCEE** (기후에너지환경부, formerly MOTIE) - runs the Basic Plan process. The 12th Basic Plan,
covering 2026-2040, is due around end-2026, mid-study. That is both the opportunity and the
risk: findings that arrive as a critique of a plan already being finalised land differently
from findings that inform an open question.

**KPX** - operates the system. Cares about balancing, ramping and curtailment as operational
realities, not as report metrics. The residual-load and flexibility results are the ones that
speak to them.

**The Presidential Secretary for Climate, Environment and Energy** - named in the contract's
short-term target indicator. Needs the argument in a page.

**National Assembly** - the visibility KPI. Needs a message that survives being repeated
without the modeller present.

## What actually resonates, and what does not

**Resonates:** curtailment, because it is wasted investment in a system where renewables are
already being curtailed in Jeju and the southwest, and everyone knows it. Regional imbalance,
because metropolitan demand and southwest generation is a live political problem. Gas
dependence and fuel-price exposure, because the import bill is a standing concern. Grid
constraint, because it is the binding limit on renewable deployment and is understood as such.

**Does not resonate:** LCOE league tables, which Korean planning already has and already
argues about. "Offshore wind is good" framing. International comparison used as an argument
rather than as context - Korea's isolated grid genuinely differs.

## Framing rules

**Exploratory, not predictive.** The contract requires it and it is also the stronger position.
"Under S2 assumptions, the model yields" invites engagement with the assumptions. "Offshore
wind will deliver" invites a fight about the forecast.

**No causal claim about policy.** The model shows what follows from a set of assumptions. It
does not show that a policy caused an outcome, and claiming so is the fastest way to lose a
technical audience.

**Comparison, not advocacy.** The study's power is that S1 versus S0 versus S2 isolates
offshore wind's contribution. Lead with the counterfactual structure, because that is what
makes the number credible.

**State the boundary before someone else does.** No LOLE, EENS or ELCC; no stability analysis;
transmission results indicative and zonal. Saying this up front reads as rigour. Having it
pointed out reads as overreach.

**Two tiers, and say why.** Reporting the conventional metrics and then showing where they
mislead is more persuasive than skipping them, because it engages the audience's existing frame
rather than dismissing it.

## The three insights (KPI-b)

At least three Korea-specific insights supported by time-series modelling. The test each must
pass: **would a Korean energy policymaker who already knows the sector find this new?**

An insight that is true and unsurprising does not satisfy the KPI. Neither does one that could
have been written without the model. Candidates worth pressing on:

- Whether offshore wind's diurnal profile actually fills the evening ramp that solar creates, and by how much.
- Whether a bad wind year hits the whole peninsula at once, which determines how much geographic aggregation helps and therefore how much firm backup a 25 GW build-out needs.
- Whether offshore wind relieves or adds to the metropolitan import constraint - the answer is not obvious and is politically consequential.
- Whether Korean surplus is shaped usefully for power-to-X, or merely large.

Name the figure that evidences each. An insight without a figure is an opinion.

## Consultations (KPI-a)

Three or more, with **documented inputs reflected in the final report**. A meeting record alone
does not satisfy this - there must be a traceable change in the report attributable to each
consultation. Plan for that when scheduling: consult while the finding is still movable.

## The Korean report

~50 pages, not a translation pass. Korean energy policy discourse has its own terminology
(계통 제약, 출력제어, 재생에너지 변동성, 유연성 자원) and using the wrong term marks the
document as translated. Maintain a bilingual glossary from the start. Where an English framing
has no natural Korean equivalent, decide deliberately rather than transliterating.
